# How to Recognize When Your Loved One Needs 24/7 Care

There's rarely a single moment when everything becomes clear. More often, it's a slow accumulation — a fall you almost didn't hear about, a skipped meal here, a forgotten medication there. If you've been quietly wondering whether your loved one needs **24/7 care**, you're not alone. Families across Silver Spring, Rockville, Gaithersburg, and the rest of Montgomery County wrestle with this question every day.

This article won't push you toward any particular decision. What it will do is give you a clear, honest framework for reading the signs — so that whatever you decide, you decide it with confidence.

---

## Why Timing Matters

Waiting too long to arrange around-the-clock support is one of the most common regrets families share. A crisis — a fall, a medical emergency, a wandering incident — can force a decision that feels rushed and overwhelming. Recognizing the warning signs early gives your family time to plan carefully, explore options, and involve your loved one in the conversation.

On the other hand, not every person who needs more help needs full-time care. That's why understanding *what* is happening — and *how often* — matters as much as the signs themselves.

---

## Physical Warning Signs

### Frequent Falls or Near-Misses

The CDC reports that falls are the leading cause of injury among older adults. One fall doesn't necessarily mean your loved one needs **24/7 care**, but a pattern of falls — or even repeated close calls — is a strong signal that solo living has become dangerous. If your loved one can't safely get up from the floor unassisted, or if they've been found on the floor after hours alone, round-the-clock support should be seriously considered.

### Struggling with Basic Daily Tasks

Pay close attention to activities of daily living (ADLs): bathing, dressing, toileting, eating, and moving from place to place. When someone can no longer manage two or more of these safely on their own, part-time help often isn't enough to fill the gaps. Risks like scalding water during bathing, choking while eating alone, or not being able to get to the bathroom in time can all become urgent safety issues.

### Medication Errors

Missing doses, doubling up, or taking the wrong medications entirely — these mistakes happen more than most families realize. Research shows that medication mismanagement is a leading cause of hospital readmissions among older adults. If your loved one is on a complex medication schedule and has a history of errors, consistent overnight or full-time oversight can be life-saving.

### Significant, Unexplained Weight Loss

When someone is losing weight without a clear medical reason, it often points to skipped meals, difficulty preparing food, or forgetting to eat altogether. This is a quiet but serious sign that daily supervision is needed.

## Cognitive and Behavioral Warning Signs

### Dementia-Related Wandering

Wandering is one of the most dangerous behaviors associated with dementia and Alzheimer's disease. The Alzheimer's Association notes that a significant percentage of people with dementia will wander at some point, and those who are not found within 24 hours face serious risk of injury or death. If your loved one has wandered — even once — **24/7 care** is no longer optional. It's urgent.

### Sundowning and Nighttime Confusion

Many people with dementia experience increased confusion, agitation, or disorientation in the late afternoon and evening hours, a pattern known as sundowning. If your loved one is up at night, attempting to leave the house in the dark, or becoming distressed during hours when no caregiver is present, daytime-only support simply isn't adequate.

### Forgetting to Turn Off Appliances

A stove left on, a faucet running for hours, a space heater left unattended — these aren't minor lapses. They're fire and flood hazards. If this is happening regularly, the risk of being home alone — even briefly — becomes too great.

### Increased Confusion About People, Places, or Time

When your loved one no longer consistently recognizes familiar faces, can't remember where they live, or loses track of what day or season it is, they've reached a stage where independent living carries real danger.

---

## Emotional and Social Warning Signs

### Withdrawal and Depression

Isolation and depression are serious health concerns for older adults — not just quality-of-life issues. Research links social isolation to a higher risk of cognitive decline, heart disease, and early mortality. If your loved one has stopped engaging with friends, family, or activities they once loved, a live-in or 24/7 caregiver can provide the consistent human connection that makes a real difference.

### Caregiver Burnout in the Family

Sometimes the clearest sign that your loved one needs **24/7 care** is the state of the family members providing informal support. If you're exhausted, anxious, unable to sleep, or feeling resentful — these aren't signs of weakness. They're signs that the current arrangement has exceeded what any one person can sustainably provide. Families in Germantown, Takoma Park, and across Maryland are increasingly turning to professional full-time care not just for their loved one's safety, but for their own.

---

## After a Medical Event

### Post-Hospitalization or Post-Surgery Recovery

A hospital discharge often marks a turning point. Older adults recovering from surgery, a stroke, a cardiac event, or a serious infection are frequently sent home before they're truly stable. The days and weeks immediately following discharge are among the highest-risk periods for complications and readmission. Around-the-clock care during this window can make the difference between a full recovery and a return trip to the hospital.

### Following a Stroke

Stroke survivors often experience sudden changes in mobility, speech, cognition, and emotional regulation. Depending on the severity, they may need close monitoring at all hours — not just for physical assistance, but to catch early signs of a second stroke or medication reaction.

## What 24/7 Care Actually Looks Like

**24/7 home care** doesn't mean a revolving door of strangers. At Vitalis HealthCare, it typically means a consistent, small team of caregivers who rotate coverage so your loved one always has someone familiar present. This can include:

- **Live-in care**, where one caregiver stays in the home and is available overnight
- **Shift-based coverage**, where caregivers work in blocks throughout the day and night
- **Skilled nursing support** layered in for complex medical needs

Care is tailored to what your loved one actually needs — not a one-size-fits-all package. Families in Silver Spring, Rockville, and surrounding communities work with our care coordinators to build a plan that fits the person, not just the diagnosis.

---

## Having the Conversation

One of the hardest parts of this process is talking to your loved one about needing more help. Most people resist the idea, at least at first. A few things that tend to help:

- **Frame it around safety, not ability.** "I want you to feel safe in your home" lands very differently than "You can't manage on your own anymore."
- **Involve them in the decision.** Ask what matters most to them — staying in their home, maintaining a routine, keeping their independence. Then show how care supports those things.
- **Start the conversation before there's a crisis.** People tend to hear these discussions better when they're not happening in a hospital waiting room.

---

If you've read this far and several of these signs feel familiar, trust that instinct. You know your loved one. The fact that you're asking the question at all usually means it's time to at least have a professional conversation about what support might look like.
