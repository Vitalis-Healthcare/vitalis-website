# Resources for Social Workers: Helping Clients Access Maryland Home Care Services

If you work as a discharge planner, medical social worker, or care coordinator in the greater Washington metro area, you already know how much rides on what happens in the first 48 hours after a patient leaves the hospital. Connecting clients with the right **Maryland home care** services — quickly and confidently — can mean the difference between a safe recovery at home and a costly readmission.

This guide is written specifically for you. It pulls together the referral pathways, funding sources, and local resources you need to help clients and their families land on their feet — whether you're working out of a Silver Spring hospital, a Rockville rehabilitation center, or a Gaithersburg outpatient clinic.

---

## Why Home Care Matters in Discharge Planning

Research consistently shows that older adults recover better in familiar environments. The home setting reduces exposure to hospital-acquired infections, supports mental and emotional well-being, and allows families to stay closely involved in care.

For social workers, the challenge isn't usually convincing a client that home is a good option — it's navigating the patchwork of services, payers, and eligibility rules to actually make it happen. The sections below are designed to cut through that complexity.

---

## Understanding the Types of Home Care Available in Maryland

Before you make a referral, it helps to be clear on what different agencies actually provide. **Maryland home care** falls into two broad categories:

### Non-Medical (Personal Care and Companion Care)
- **Companion care** — social engagement, light housekeeping, meal preparation, medication reminders, and transportation
- **Personal care** — hands-on help with bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, and mobility
- **Live-in or 24-hour care** — continuous coverage for clients who cannot be safely left alone

These services are provided by home health aides and companion caregivers. They do not require a physician's order and can begin quickly — often within 24 to 48 hours of a referral.

### Skilled Home Health
- **Skilled nursing visits** — wound care, medication management, injections, health monitoring
- **Physical, occupational, or speech therapy** — typically ordered by a physician post-discharge
- **Medication reconciliation** — especially critical in the first weeks after hospitalization

Skilled home health is typically covered by Medicare Part A after a qualifying hospital stay and requires a physician's order along with a face-to-face encounter. Non-medical home care is a separate benefit and is not covered the same way — this is a distinction families often misunderstand, and clarifying it early will save everyone time.

## Funding Sources: What to Know Before the Referral

### Medicare
Medicare covers **skilled home health** services (nursing, therapy) for homebound clients following a qualifying stay, but it does not cover ongoing personal care or companion services. Make sure families understand this gap before they leave the hospital.

### Maryland Medicaid
Maryland's Medicaid program offers several pathways to **home and community-based services** for eligible clients:

- **Community Personal Assistance Services (CPAS)** — covers personal care for Medicaid recipients who meet functional eligibility criteria
- **Community First Choice (CFC)** — a Medicaid waiver option that supports personal care and more for individuals who would otherwise qualify for nursing facility level of care
- **Rare and Expensive Case Management (REM) Waiver and Brain Injury Waiver** — for clients with specific diagnoses
- **HealthChoice Managed Care Organizations (MCOs)** — most Maryland Medicaid enrollees are in an MCO; contact the specific MCO's care coordinator for authorization

Eligibility and service authorization can take time. Whenever possible, initiate Medicaid referrals before discharge, not after.

### Private Pay
Many families in Montgomery County and surrounding areas choose private-pay home care, particularly when they need services to start immediately or when they want a specific caregiver or schedule that Medicaid-funded programs may not accommodate. Be ready to discuss realistic cost expectations and ask whether the family has long-term care insurance — many clients don't realize they have coverage until someone asks.

### Long-Term Care Insurance
If a client has a long-term care insurance policy, review the benefit triggers (usually inability to perform two or more ADLs, or cognitive impairment). Many policies require care to be provided by a licensed agency. Agencies operated to **Joint Commission standards**, like Vitalis HealthCare, often satisfy insurer requirements more readily than independent caregivers.

## Key Maryland and Montgomery County Resources

These agencies and programs are worth having bookmarked:

- **Maryland Department of Aging** — administers the Medicaid waivers and coordinates with local Area Agencies on Aging; maryland.gov/aging
- **Montgomery County Aging and Disability Services** — local point of entry for older adults and adults with disabilities in the county; 240-777-3000
- **Maryland Access Point (MAP)** — a network of local one-stop hubs that help older adults, adults with disabilities, and caregivers find services; a warm handoff to MAP is often the fastest way to start a Medicaid evaluation
- **211 Maryland** — for clients who need help covering immediate needs (food, transportation, utilities) while longer-term care is being arranged
- **Agespan / Jewish Council for the Aging (JCA)** — offers care management, caregiver support, and navigation services across Montgomery County

## Making the Referral: What a Good Agency Needs From You

When you refer to a **Maryland home care** agency, the intake process goes faster when you can provide:

- **Diagnosis and functional summary** — what can the client do independently? Where are the gaps?
- **Anticipated discharge date and address** — especially important for clients in Silver Spring, Rockville, Germantown, Takoma Park, or other parts of Montgomery County where traffic and geography affect caregiver availability
- **Payer source** — private pay, Medicaid MCO (and which MCO), long-term care insurance, or veteran's benefits
- **Emergency contacts and family involvement** — is there a family caregiver at home? Will they need training?
- **Safety concerns** — fall risk, wandering risk, medication mismanagement, unsafe home environment

A reputable agency will conduct its own intake assessment, but the more context you provide up front, the smoother the transition.

## Red Flags to Watch for in Agency Selection

Not all home care agencies operate at the same standard. When helping clients choose, encourage families to ask:

- Is the agency **licensed by the Maryland Department of Health OHCQ**?
- Are caregivers employees of the agency (with taxes withheld, workers' comp coverage, and liability insurance), or are they independent contractors?
- Does the agency conduct **criminal background checks** and **reference verification** on all caregivers?
- Is there a supervisor clients and families can reach after hours?
- Does the agency have a track record with clients who have **dementia, stroke recovery, post-surgical needs**, or other complex conditions?

Agencies that are family-owned and community-based — like Vitalis HealthCare, based in Silver Spring — often provide more consistent caregiver assignments and more responsive communication than large franchises. That consistency matters enormously for clients with cognitive impairment or complex care needs.

## Supporting Family Caregivers in Your Plan

The client isn't the only one who needs support. Research shows that family caregivers are at significant risk for burnout, depression, and their own health decline. When you build a discharge plan, consider whether the informal caregiver at home also needs:

- **Respite care** — scheduled breaks from caregiving
- **Caregiver education** — safe transfer techniques, medication management, recognizing warning signs
- **Emotional support referrals** — support groups through JCA, AARP, or Alzheimer's Association

Building caregiver support into the plan from day one reduces the likelihood of a breakdown that sends the client back to the hospital.
