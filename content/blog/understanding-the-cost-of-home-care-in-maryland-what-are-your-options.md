# Understanding the Cost of Home Care in Maryland: What Are Your Options?

When a family member starts needing extra help at home, one of the first questions that comes up is: *what is this actually going to cost?* The **cost of home care in Maryland** isn't one-size-fits-all, and that can feel overwhelming when you're already dealing with the emotional weight of watching someone you love need more support. The good news is that once you understand what drives pricing — and what payment options exist — you'll feel a lot more in control of the decision.

This article walks you through everything you need to know, from how agencies calculate rates to the real-world options families in Silver Spring, Rockville, Gaithersburg, Germantown, and across Montgomery County use to make home care work for their budget.

---

## What Does Home Care Actually Cost in Maryland?

Home care is typically priced **by the hour**, though some agencies offer flat daily or weekly rates for higher-hour cases. Rates in Maryland vary depending on the level of care, the agency's credentials, and where in the state you live.

Generally speaking, you can expect:

- **Companion care and homemaking** (light housekeeping, errands, conversation, meal prep) tends to be on the lower end of the hourly range.
- **Personal care** (bathing, dressing, grooming, mobility assistance) is typically priced a step higher because caregivers require more specific training.
- **Skilled nursing services** (wound care, medication management, health monitoring by licensed nurses) carry the highest hourly rates, reflecting the clinical expertise involved.

According to national research and industry surveys, Maryland consistently ranks among the higher-cost states for home care — largely because of the cost of living in areas like Montgomery County and the competitive labor market for qualified caregivers. Families in Takoma Park, Rockville, and Germantown should factor this regional reality into their planning.

### Minimum Hours and Scheduling

Most licensed home care agencies in Maryland have a **minimum shift requirement** — often two to four hours per visit. This matters when you're budgeting, especially if your loved one only needs occasional help. Some families are surprised that scheduling a one-hour check-in may not be an option through a licensed agency. That structure exists to protect both clients and caregivers, and it ensures consistency of care.

## What Affects the Price of Home Care?

Several factors influence what you'll actually pay week to week:

- **Level of care needed** — Companion care costs less than personal care, which costs less than skilled nursing.
- **Hours per week** — Families needing round-the-clock or live-in care often access better per-hour rates than those scheduling a few hours a week.
- **Time of day and day of week** — Evening, overnight, weekend, and holiday hours typically carry a higher rate.
- **Location** — Rates in Gaithersburg or Silver Spring may differ slightly from more rural parts of Maryland.
- **Agency credentials and oversight** — A licensed, insured agency with trained caregivers and supervision built in costs more than an independent caregiver hired privately. That difference reflects real protections for your family.

## How Do Families Pay for Home Care in Maryland?

This is where most families have more options than they realize. Here's an honest look at the most common payment paths.

### Private Pay (Out of Pocket)

**Private pay** is the most straightforward option. Families pay the agency directly, on an ongoing or as-needed basis. While this requires liquid funds or careful budgeting, it also gives you the most flexibility — you choose the hours, the schedule, and the level of care without waiting for approvals.

Many families use a combination of savings, retirement income, or proceeds from a home sale to fund private pay care. A trusted elder law attorney or financial planner can help you structure assets to make this sustainable.

### Long-Term Care Insurance

If your loved one purchased **long-term care (LTC) insurance** earlier in life, now is the time to use it. Most LTC policies cover licensed home care, including personal care and skilled nursing. Policies vary widely in their benefit amounts, elimination periods (the waiting period before benefits kick in), and inflation protections.

It's worth pulling out the policy documents and calling the insurance company directly — or working with a benefits coordinator — to understand exactly what's covered and how to submit claims.

### Medicaid Waiver Programs

For families with limited income and assets, Maryland's **Medicaid waiver programs** can cover home care services that allow your loved one to stay in their home rather than move to a nursing facility. The main programs to know are:

- **Community First Choice (CFC)** — Covers personal care and related support for Medicaid-eligible individuals.
- **Community Personal Assistance Services (CPAS)** — Another Medicaid option for eligible seniors and adults with disabilities.
- **Older Adults Waiver** — Designed specifically for adults 55 and older who would otherwise need nursing home-level care.

Applying for Medicaid waivers takes time and documentation, so starting the process early — before a crisis — is strongly advised. Our article on [Medicaid and Medicare coverage for home care in Maryland](/blog/a-guide-to-medicaid-and-medicare-coverage-for-home-care-in-maryland) walks through eligibility and the application process in more detail.

### Medicare

**Medicare** covers home health care only under specific conditions — primarily skilled nursing, physical therapy, or occupational therapy ordered by a physician following a hospitalization or for management of a health condition. It does **not** cover ongoing personal care or companion care on its own.

If your loved one has just been discharged from a hospital or rehab facility in Rockville, Gaithersburg, or anywhere in Montgomery County, it's worth confirming what Medicare will and won't cover during that transition period.

### Veterans Benefits

If your loved one served in the military, they may be eligible for home care assistance through the **VA Aid & Attendance** benefit or other VA programs. These benefits are often underused simply because families don't know they exist. A Veterans Service Organization (VSO) can help you navigate the application process at no cost.

### Reverse Mortgages and Life Insurance Options

Some families explore a **reverse mortgage** to convert home equity into funds for care — this works best when the senior plans to remain in the home long-term. Others look at **life insurance policy loans or accelerated death benefits**, which allow policyholders with certain diagnoses to access a portion of their death benefit while still living. These are complex financial instruments, and we'd always recommend speaking with a qualified financial advisor before moving forward.

## How to Think About Value, Not Just Cost

It's easy to focus only on the hourly rate when comparing options, but **the true cost of home care includes what you'd pay without it**. Emergency room visits from a preventable fall, the toll of family caregiver burnout, or the transition to assisted living when a loved one could have safely remained home — these have real financial and emotional costs too.

Research consistently shows that most older adults strongly prefer to age in place. Supporting that preference, when it's safe and feasible, often turns out to be both the right choice *and* the cost-effective one over time.

When evaluating an agency, look beyond the hourly rate:

- Is the agency **licensed by the Maryland Department of Health OHCQ**?
- Are caregivers **background-checked, trained, and supervised**?
- Does the agency carry **liability insurance and workers' compensation**?
- Are they available to respond when something changes at 2 a.m.?

These aren't small details — they're what separates a safe, reliable care arrangement from one that leaves your family exposed.
